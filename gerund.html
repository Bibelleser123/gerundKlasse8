📘 The Gerund – Verb + -ing

1. Reading: Kids in the USA

Life in the USA is different for every kid, but many things are the same everywhere. Meeting friends after school is one of the most popular activities. Many American kids enjoy playing sports like basketball, baseball, or soccer. Playing outside with friends makes them happy.

Emma lives in Chicago. She loves swimming in the lake during summer. She is very good at swimming, and she is thinking of joining a swim team next year. Her brother Jake is more interested in playing video games. He spends hours playing his favorite games, but his mom doesn't like him spending so much time in front of the screen. She keeps telling him to go outside more.

Doing homework is not always fun, but most kids don't mind doing it if they can listen to music at the same time. Some students are afraid of failing a test, so they spend a lot of time studying. Others are more relaxed about studying because they know that trying hard is the most important thing.

After school, many kids look forward to hanging out with their friends. Hanging out at the mall or at a friend's house is very common. Some kids are famous for organizing fun activities for their friends, like movie nights or game nights.

Eating healthy food is important, but many American kids admit eating too much fast food. Parents often suggest eating more fruit and vegetables, but children keep choosing pizza and burgers instead.

Using social media is a big part of many teenagers' lives. Being interested in chatting with friends online is normal, but some parents worry about their kids spending too much time on their phones. Instead of watching videos all evening, some families prefer playing board games together.

In summer, many families go on vacation. Traveling to new places is exciting for most kids. Some children love visiting national parks and hiking in the mountains. Others prefer relaxing at the beach and building sandcastles.

There's no point in worrying about school during vacation! It's worth trying new things and making new memories. By the end of summer, most kids are looking forward to seeing their friends again at school, even if they don't like getting up early in the morning.

All highlighted words are gerunds (verb + -ing used like a noun).

2. Grammar Explanation

What is a gerund?
The gerund is a verb form. You make it like this: verb + -ing (just like the present participle).
Spelling rules: try → trying, live → living, get → getting.
A gerund is used like a noun in the sentence.

a) Gerund as the subject of the sentence

The gerund can stand at the beginning of the sentence, just like a noun.

Meeting up isn't easy right now.
Doing homework is not always fun.
Traveling to new places is exciting.

b) Gerund as the object after certain verbs

Some verbs are always followed by a gerund, never by "to + verb". These are often verbs about liking or not liking something: enjoy, love, like, hate, avoid, finish, imagine, keep, miss, suggest, don't mind, admit.

Matt enjoys being in Pittsburgh.
Jake spends hours playing video games.
Parents suggest eating more vegetables.

Careful! After like, dislike, love, prefer, start both forms are possible with no difference in meaning: She started running. / She started to run.

Very careful! After would like / would love you always use to + verb (infinitive), never the gerund! I'd like to watch a video now.

c) Gerund after prepositions

PatternExample from the text
Adjective + preposition + gerundJake's mom is not happy about him spending time on games. Emma is good at swimming.
Noun + preposition + gerundSome kids are famous for organizing fun activities. Emma is thinking of joining a team.
Verb + preposition + gerundMany kids look forward to hanging out with friends. Parents worry about their kids spending too much time online.

Common combinations: afraid of, tired of, interested in, good at, scared of, famous for, crazy about, keen on, look forward to, think about, dream of, feel like, worry about, talk about, get used to.

Careful! "look forward to" and "get used to" – here to is a preposition, so a gerund follows: She's looking forward to seeing her friends.

d) Gerund after prepositions in adverbial phrases

Prepositions like by, without, instead of, in spite of are followed by a gerund when they describe how, why or under what circumstances something happens.

He got a scholarship by studying hard.
She answered without thinking.
Instead of watching videos, they played a board game.

e) Gerund after fixed phrases

After these common phrases, the gerund must be used: It's no use ..., There's no point in ..., It's (not) worth ..., How/What about ...?

There's no point in worrying about school during vacation.
It's worth trying new things.
How about meeting up later?
